supragnosis v0.7.1

Upgrade the app and the server, then restart once; the title bar moves the window again.

Changes since v0.7.0.

Upgrade the app and the server, then restart once; the title bar moves the window again. brew upgrade, then supragnosis restart. Only the app changed; the server is rebuilt with the version, and a restart keeps This Mac from offering the update. The store, the sync protocol, the MCP tools and status --json are unchanged.

The title bar moves the window

v0.7.0's title bar became a Mac toolbar, and its two groups cover all of the bar but its padding. The app marked only the bar itself as the window's drag handle, and a press counts only on the element that carries the mark, so the window moved from a sliver at the bar's edges. The whole bar is the handle now, except its buttons, links and the search field; a double-click zooms the window, and the search's results, which open inside the bar, take their own clicks. The starting screen and the remote server's page, whose text covers their middle, drag the same way.

Restart says what is true

Settings > This Mac > Restart, on a daemon the login job runs, could report a failure that had already passed and keep showing it. The app ran supragnosis restart and at once looked for the daemon. When launchd was still starting the new process, or retrying one that lost the store's lock while the old one exited, the app started a daemon of its own, which died on the lock, and the app went on saying the daemon had failed. Nothing read the state again: the tray's line stayed on the failure, and This Mac stayed "Not running" until the window was focused again.

The app now waits up to 30 seconds for the daemon to answer before it says what the restart came to, and starts no daemon of its own beside a login job. A refusal that left the same process says why; one after which a new process answers is a restart. A failure is not kept once the daemon answers: the next time the app reads the daemon's state, it attaches again. This Mac reads the state every two seconds after a restart until the daemon answers, for up to a minute, and a refused restart shows as a failure, not as "Daemon restarted".
