Changes since v0.7.2.

**Upgrade the server and the app, then restart once; the map opens and moves at any size.**
`brew upgrade`, then `supragnosis restart`. The viewer changed, and so did the daemon's graph routes
and the app's proxy, which now carries a poll's tag; an older app still works, but asks for the
whole graph on every poll. The store, the sync protocol, the MCP tools and `status --json` are
unchanged.

## A store of 10,000 opens in two seconds

A store of 10,000 entities, entered through observe and opened in the desktop app, did not show in
six minutes. The drawing was not why. Such a store holds a context for every observation - 11,862 on
that one - and the layout pulled and pushed every context of two or more, building each one's
members again every step. Every 2.5 seconds the page fetched and parsed the whole graph, the
hypergraph and the Log's every observation, about 21 MB, whether anything had changed or not. And
the layout stepped on the page's own thread, inside each frame.

- **The poll asks first.** The graph, the hypergraph and the Log answer with an `ETag`, and a poll
  that names it gets a `304` with no body; the page keeps what it has. The app's proxy passes the
  tag both ways.
- **The Log lists the newest 200 observations**, says "200+" while there are more, and shows 200
  more on request.
- **The regions' forces grow with their members.** Membership is kept as index arrays between steps,
  and only the regions the map draws, three or more members, push apart; every context still pulls.
- **The layout runs on a thread of its own**, in a worker started from `viewer-sim.js`, and on the
  page's thread only where no worker starts. Its positions are the old step's, to the last bit.
- **A fresh layout starts from its communities**, found by label propagation and laid out first, so
  it reveals in about 60 steps rather than 120.

Through its own daemon that store now sends its data in 0.3 seconds and shows in about two.

## Every frame does less

- **A frame that only redraws does not step the layout**, and a layout at rest visits no pair of
  nodes.
- **The layout's forces come from grids**: near forces pair by pair in a node's own and adjacent
  cells, the far field per cell, and regions push only near each other.
- **A frame touches what the window holds**: a grid of positions and of relations' midpoints answers
  what is drawn, named, badged, pulsing or under the pointer.
- **Layers that only move with the camera are not worked out again**: the region field is kept and
  moved as an image until the camera rests, and the minimap keeps its miniature as a bitmap.
- **A node's detail follows its size on the screen without a step**: its halo, its trust ring, the
  arrowheads into it and the bend of its lanes grow in as it grows, eased in 32 steps.
- **The GPU draws the map's bulk** where WebGL2 can render to half-float targets: the region field,
  the workspace outlines, the relations and the nodes. Names, badges and peers stay on the 2D canvas
  above it, and the 2D canvas draws everything as before without WebGL2, under forced colors, when
  the context is lost, and with `?renderer=2d`.

## Zoomed out, the map keeps its best-connected nodes

A large map zoomed out was one dark disc. It now keeps whole about one node for each 12 by 12 pixels
of the screen it covers, ranked by their relations, and the next as many fade out, so the
least-connected fade first and zooming in brings them back where they were. A node that has faded is
not drawn, named or under the pointer, and a far frame visits only the nodes it keeps. Every node
stays where the layout put it: nothing is drawn for nodes that faded, and no line for relations two
nodes do not have. A hovered, selected, active, pulsing or contested node never fades. The minimap
keeps its nodes the same way at its own scale, with only its widest regions, and paints a map of
50,000 in under 3 ms where it took 81.

## Measured

On an M3 Max, on a graph shaped like a store, on the GPU (design-system.md Section 27.2):

| | 10,000 Chrome | 10,000 WebKit | 50,000 Chrome | 50,000 WebKit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| First picture | 2.2 s | 2.1 s | 8.8 s | 7.2 s |
| A frame at rest, 95th percentile | 9.7 ms | 10 ms | 8.1 ms | 11 ms |
| A frame while the layout moves, 95th percentile | 8.7 ms | 10 ms | 19.4 ms | 28 ms |

Before this release the viewer took 100 seconds to show 10,000 entities and 651 ms a frame to pan
them, on the bench's first graph (design-system.md Section 26.2). At 50,000 a frame while the layout
settles is still over 16.7 ms; at rest it is within it.

## For people who build on it

- `GET /api/graph`, `/api/hypergraph` and `/api/observations` answer with a strong `ETag` and answer
  a matching `If-None-Match` with `304 Not Modified`.
- The page loads two more scripts from the daemon, `/viewer-sim.js` and `/viewer-gl.js`; the content
  policy is unchanged, since a worker from a served file is `'self'`.
- `scenarios/web/bench/canvas.mjs` measures the map: `SIZES`, `BROWSER` and `RENDERER=2d` choose the
  run (design-system.md Section 26.1).
