# Languages - the product's pages and the site in English and Korean

> Which language a page shows, where its words are kept, and what stays in English. Companion to
> [design-system.md](design-system.md), whose faces (design-system.md Section 4), words
> (design-system.md Section 9), site pages (design-system.md Section 11 and design-system.md
> Section 11a) and invariants (design-system.md Section 13) this extends, and to
> [recall.md](recall.md) Section 5, which is why the agents' side stays in English.
>
> Status: **specified, not built**. The scope (D1) was chosen by the author on 2026-11-06.

## 1. Why this exists

Every page is in English by decision: design-system.md Section 9 said "The interface stays in
English", and design-system.md Section 12 kept "the same English words everywhere". On 2026-11-06
the author asked for Korean beside English, for the product's pages and the site.

What a person reads today, measured on 2026-11-06 as lines that hold an English phrase:

| Surface | Where | Lines |
|---|---|---|
| The viewer (the Graph and Review pages) | `crates/supragnosis-viz/assets/viewer/*.js`, `viewer.html` | about 230 |
| The app's pages (Settings, Setup) | `app/assets/settings.js`, `welcome.js`, `kit.js`, `shell-init.js` | about 150 |
| The app's Rust: menu, tray, splash, status lines, notices | `app/src/main.rs`, `menu.rs`, `tray.rs`, `splash.rs`, `daemon.rs`, `apps.rs`, `welcome.rs` | about 95 |
| The landing and the Privacy page | `site/index.html`, `site/privacy/index.html` | about 140 |
| The principles page | `site/principles/index.html`, `site/principles.js`, and the regions the coverage registry writes into it | about 450, mostly prose |
| Words other programs send a page | the engine's proposal and check words, `setup`'s events, refusals | closed sets and open sentences |

Every string is written where it is used. Dates are formatted two ways: Settings fixes `en-US`, the
viewer takes the system's default. The viewer, the landing's stage and the principles page draw
text on canvases, and the site's headings are set in Fraunces, which has no Hangul.

- **D1, the scope** (the author, 2026-11-06): the viewer, the app's pages, menu and tray, and the
  site's landing, principles and Privacy pages.

## 2. What this is NOT

- **Not the agents' language.** The MCP server's instructions, the tools' descriptions, the
  prompts, the hook's index and its commit question stay in English. The records are written in
  English, and English queries find them: in recall.md Section 5's eval, an English query named in
  its workspace found its record within five hits 0.93 of the time, and a Korean one 0.62.
- **Not a translation of what the store holds.** An observation's content, an entity's name, a
  type, a workspace's name and a host's id are shown as written, in whatever language they were
  written in.
- **Not the CLI, and not the news pages.** The command line's output, `setup`'s own sentences
  included, and the release notes on `/news/` stay in English (Section 9).
- **Not a run-time translator.** Every Korean string is written by hand into the words of its
  surface and reviewed like any other text.

## 3. Which language a page shows (D2)

- **The app follows macOS, and says so to its pages.** The app's pages, its menu and its tray show
  the language macOS gives the app: the person's preferred languages, or the one picked for this
  app in System Settings > General > Language & Region > Applications, which lists an app once its
  bundle declares more than one localization (`CFBundleLocalizations`: `en`, `ko`). The app reads
  it once, from the bundle's preferred localization, and hands it to every page it opens in the
  user script it already injects (`shell-init.js`), with the shell's own words ("this Mac", the
  gear's name). No page guesses it from `navigator.languages`. The app has no language setting of
  its own, as it has no appearance setting of its own (U27).
- **A browser decides for a page opened in it.** The viewer opened in a browser shows the first of
  the browser's languages that is `ko` or `en`, and English when neither is listed.
- **The site's language is in its path.** English stays at `/`, `/principles/` and `/privacy/`;
  Korean is at `/ko/`, `/ko/principles/` and `/ko/privacy/`. Nothing redirects by the browser's
  language: the site is files on GitHub Pages, and a script that redirects breaks Back and what a
  search engine indexes. Each page links its twin by the other language's own name ("한국어",
  "English"), names it as `<link rel="alternate" hreflang>`, and has its own canonical address and
  `og:locale`.
- **A page declares what it shows.** `<html lang>` is `en` or `ko`: written in a site page's file;
  on a product page in the app, set by the shell's user script, which runs as the document starts;
  on the viewer in a browser, set by its words when they run. A passage
  in the other language - the registry's clauses on the Korean principles page - carries its own
  `lang`; a name or a term does not need one (WCAG 3.1.2).

## 4. Where the words are kept (D3, D4)

- **One file of words per surface, both languages in each entry.** An entry is a key and its two
  strings, `{ en: "...", ko: "..." }`, so a missing or stale translation sits next to its English:
  - the viewer: `crates/supragnosis-viz/assets/viewer/00-words.js`, joined first by the build;
  - the app: `app/assets/words.json`, one file for its Rust and its pages, so a daemon state keeps
    one word on the tray, the status line and the Settings card (U17). The Rust embeds it; the shell
    hands a page the words of its language with the language itself;
  - the site's scripts: a table in `stage.js` and one in `principles.js`, keyed by the page's
    `lang`;
  - the principles page's generated regions: a table beside the generator in the coverage test,
    which writes both pages.
- **Markup marks its words.** A product page's markup holds its English and marks each string with
  its key (`data-t` for text, `data-t-label` for `aria-label` and `title`). The words run first
  among the page's scripts at the end of its body - the viewer's as the first part of the joined
  `viewer.js`, the app pages' as the first script they load - and fill the marked strings before any
  other script reads the page, since `01-state.js` and the app pages' scripts read the markup as
  they start. Serving a page per language from the daemon was set aside: it would move the words
  into Rust and the build.
- **The site's pages are two files each.** The landing, the principles page and the Privacy page
  are hand-written HTML, so the Korean page is its own file under `site/ko/`, written from the
  English one with the same skeleton (LG4). A generator that writes both from one template was set
  aside: it would turn hand-written pages into a build step for what the skeleton check already
  holds. The principles page's cases are data, not words, and are the same in both files.
- **A sentence is one entry.** A page looks a sentence up with `t(key, values)` and named
  placeholders, `{name}`; it never builds a sentence by joining entries, since Korean puts the verb
  last and the parts in another order. Both strings of an entry carry the same placeholders.
- **Plurals and numbers through `Intl`.** An English entry may hold `one` and `other`; Korean holds
  `other` alone, and the page picks with `Intl.PluralRules`. Numbers, dates and times are formatted
  with `Intl` in the page's language, so Settings' fixed `en-US` goes.
- **No particle after a placeholder.** Korean's particles change with the last sound of the word
  before them (을/를, 이/가, 은/는), and a name in Latin letters has no reliable last sound. A Korean
  string is phrased so a placeholder is followed by a space, a quote or a noun:
  "'{name}' 항목을 병합했습니다", not "{name}을 병합했습니다". There is no helper that guesses a
  particle.
- **How it is checked (D8).** Each file and table of words is read by a test: every entry has both
  strings, the same placeholders in each, and no particle after a placeholder. A test that holds a
  page's words today reads them from the words instead of the script. A string written outside the
  words is a review question, like what a sentence claims; nothing scans the scripts for English.

## 5. Words other programs send a page (D5)

- **A word from a closed set is the page's.** Where another program picks a word from a closed set,
  it sends the set's id and the page says it in its own words:
  - the engine's `proposal_words` gives the Log a proposal's noun and what happened to it ("Merge
    proposed") from the proposal's kind, its event and a verdict's decision; the page looks the
    words up by those ids, and the check that every combination has a sentence (U17) reads the
    page's words against `PROPOSAL_KINDS`, `ProposalEventKind::ALL` and `VERDICT_DECISIONS`;
  - a check's name, a trust rung and a daemon state are said the same way;
  - a `setup` event that a page draws or reads - a step that wants `--replace` or `--take-over`,
    the model's download progress - carries a `code` and its values beside its `say` (its `id`
    already names the AI app), so the page
    neither shows `say` as a card's sentence nor parses it. O4 holds: the states still come from
    the events.
- **A sentence written for one case stays its writer's.** A refusal's reason (U1) and a check's
  detail are sentences the server writes for that case, and are shown in English inside a notice
  or a row whose frame is the page's words. A command's own output on Setup's log is the CLI's, and
  stays English with it. Both are owed (Section 9).

## 6. The words themselves (D6)

Korean follows the macOS Korean interface: a menu item, a button or a tab is a noun or a short
verb stem ("설정...", "취소", "완료"), a sentence ends in 합니다, and a question asks with
"-하겠습니까?". The product is "Supragnosis" in both languages, never spelled in Hangul, and
"supragnosis" in a command (D17). A Mac is "이 Mac", as macOS says it, and another machine "이 기기".

new shared term is added here before it is used on two surfaces:

## 7. Faces for Korean (D7)

- **A page that can show Korean carries all of Pretendard.** The author's dynamic subset is 92
  files, each with a `unicode-range`, so a page fetches only the files its text needs; the viewer
  already serves all of them. The app's pages and the Korean site pages carry all 92 as well: the
  app's bundle grows by about 3MB, and `site/fonts/` holds its own copy, as it holds its nine
  today. The English site pages keep the nine files that hold Latin.
- **Korean headings are set in Pretendard.** Fraunces has no Hangul, and a heading that took its
  Latin from Fraunces and its Hangul from another face would be two faces on one line, so under
  `:lang(ko)` the site's headings take Pretendard at 700. The wordmark keeps its face.
- **A site canvas waits for its face.** The landing's stage and the principles page's scenes ask
  `document.fonts.load` for a Hangul sample before their first frame on a Korean page, since a
  canvas does not make the browser fetch a file a `unicode-range` holds back. The viewer already
  draws its labels again when a face arrives.
- **The social image stays English.** The Korean pages name the English `og.png` (Section 9).

## 8. Ordering

Each step is one PR of about 500 lines, tests aside.

0. **This document** and the sentences of design-system.md it revises (Section 10).
1. **The landing and the Privacy page in Korean**: `site/ko/`, the twin links and `hreflang`, the
   faces and the headings, `stage.js`'s table, the skeleton check, and `pages.yml` stamping the
   version into both landings.
2. **The principles page in Korean**, in two parts if its prose needs them. The generator writes
   its regions into both pages: their labels from its table in the page's language, the clauses in
   the registry's own English words, marked `lang="en"` (U12, U13).
3. **The app**: `app/assets/words.json`, the language read from the bundle and handed to the pages,
   `CFBundleLocalizations`, and a menu bar built from the words instead of `Menu::default`, its
   standard items titled from them and its menus found by id rather than by their English titles;
   `setup`'s events gain the codes Section 5 gives them.
4. **The viewer**, in two parts: the map, its panels and search first; the Log, Review, notices and
   the inspector second, with the words that move from the engine (Section 5).

## 9. Revisit when

- **The CLI's output:** when a person's first run goes through the command line rather than the
  app's Setup.
- **The news pages:** when a release note is written for Korean readers.
- **A refusal's reason or a check's detail in Korean:** when a Korean reader reports one they could
  not read; it would give each an id the page can phrase.
- **A Korean social image:** when the Korean pages are shared.
- **A language setting in the app:** when macOS's per-app language does not serve someone.
- **A third language:** when someone asks for one.
